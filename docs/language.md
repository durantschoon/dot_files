The term in **Use** is the one to write now. A later row may replace it again.

| 2026-11-19 | `183904a500364c68bbc04a41b61f688595da110b` | `HOMEBASE_GUIX` | `HOMEBASE` (for the guix sense) | `HOMEBASE` named the CONTAINER, which left nothing to name the machine. Now `HOMEBASE` is the MACHINE (ssh target, uptime, the fallback for anything that just needs a host) and `HOMEBASE_GUIX` is where guix work happens (the emacs daemon, the hygiene jobs). `HOMEBASE_GUIX ?= $(HOMEBASE)`, so a home base that runs guix natively drops the env entry and the two collapse with no rename -- which is why not `HOMEBASE_CONTAINER` (names the mechanism, the part meant to disappear) or `HOMEBASE_MACHINE` (a second name for one host, outliving the Mac that forced the split). **To read a hash:** before `c2ee336bf9c81cd12e8dd9c928ce04e301721539` (2026-10-21) neither variable existed and the host was hardcoded (`EMACS_HOST ?= minius`); from `c2ee336` through `183904a^` `HOMEBASE` meant `orb-guix`, the container -- it was born with that meaning and never denoted the machine, so the Makefile comment reading "orb-guix; previously minius" referred to the hardcoded default, not to this variable; `HOME_BASE` was an alias over that span until `938d26c77733f367432d9f8ab3c9c30aca1e2553` removed it. From `183904a` onward, `HOMEBASE` means the machine. |
